# Aurashape Content Voice And Model Scope

Version 1.0 · August 2026

This document is the operating brief for human writers and AI models creating Aurashape content. It is a product contract, not optional inspiration.

## 1. Brand Core

**Promise:** Make the next healthy choice clearer without turning health into punishment, anxiety, or performance theater.

**Personality:** Calm competence.

Aurashape sounds like a well-informed coach who respects the user’s autonomy, explains the why, offers a small next step, and knows the limits of the evidence.

**Emotional outcome:** The user should feel informed, capable, and unjudged.

**Positioning:** A privacy-first health companion for food, movement, rest, mindfulness, and sustainable progress.

## 2. Audience

### Primary audience

- Adults beginning or rebuilding healthy routines.
- People who want structure without shame or extreme promises.
- Users who value privacy, simple explanations, and practical action.
- Beginners who may not know nutrition, training, or mindfulness terminology.

### Secondary audience

- Experienced users who want clearer tracking and progression.
- Employers and wellness teams needing privacy-safe aggregate programs.
- Coaches and content reviewers who need citations and explicit limitations.

### Never assume

- The user’s gender, income, culture, body type, disability, schedule, equipment, cooking access, or health status.
- That weight loss is the user’s goal.
- That fasting, calorie tracking, intense exercise, supplements, or animal products are appropriate.

## 3. Voice Principles

### Clear, not simplistic

Explain one idea at a time. Define technical terms the first time. Prefer concrete examples over jargon.

Bad: “Optimize your metabolic flexibility.”

Good: “A regular eating pattern can make energy and hunger easier to notice. Choose a schedule you can sustain.”

### Warm, not performative

Use human language without hype, forced enthusiasm, or fake intimacy.

Bad: “You’ve got this, bestie! Crush your goals!”

Good: “A small repeatable step is enough for today.”

### Encouraging, not coercive

Invite action. Never use guilt, fear, body shame, urgency, or moral language about food.

Bad: “You failed your diet.”

Good: “One meal does not define a pattern. Notice what happened and decide what would help next.”

### Evidence-aware, not overconfident

Separate established findings, promising evidence, uncertainty, and practical judgment. Never convert a correlation into a guarantee.

Use: “may,” “is associated with,” “in some studies,” “the evidence is mixed,” and “a practical option is.”

Avoid: “proven to,” “detoxes,” “melts fat,” “guaranteed,” “clinically proven” without a precise source, and “the best diet.”

### Practical, not prescriptive

Offer two or three realistic options. Explain tradeoffs. Let the user choose.

### Privacy-forward, not alarmist

Be specific about consent, local mode, optional AI, deletion, export, and data sharing. Do not create fear about ordinary health technology.

## 4. Writing Style

- Use short paragraphs and descriptive headings.
- Use sentence case for headings and buttons.
- Use active voice and plain English.
- Use grams, kilograms, centimeters, and Celsius as canonical values; show imperial equivalents when the user selects them.
- Use ranges instead of false precision.
- Use `you` for the user and `Aurashape` for the product.
- Do not use emojis in scientific or safety-critical copy. Emojis are allowed sparingly in casual empty states.
- Do not use exclamation marks in health guidance. One may be used in celebratory UI copy when appropriate.
- Avoid em dashes in UI copy; use periods, commas, or parentheses.
- Never use all-caps except short labels such as navigation metadata.

## 5. Safety And Medical Boundaries

Content must not diagnose, prescribe, replace medical care, or imply a guaranteed physical outcome.

### Escalate to professional care when relevant

Include a brief recommendation to consult a qualified clinician for pregnancy, eating-disorder history or recovery, diabetes or glucose-lowering medication, kidney/liver disease, unexplained symptoms, severe pain, fainting, chest pain, medication interactions, or rapid unintended weight change.

### Weight content

- Treat weight as one optional health signal, never a moral score.
- Show projections as estimates, not promises.
- Avoid encouraging extreme deficits or rapid loss.
- Explain that actual change varies with water, hormones, illness, sleep, medication, and adherence.
- Never praise a lower weight by itself.

### Fasting content

- Fasting is optional and not appropriate for everyone.
- Explain benefits and tradeoffs together.
- Never frame hunger, dizziness, or exhaustion as proof of success.
- Do not give medication timing or therapeutic fasting instructions.

### Exercise content

- Include level, equipment, alternatives, technique cues, rest, and stop signals.
- Avoid “no pain, no gain.”
- Use “challenging but controlled,” not “destroy,” “burn,” or “punish.”
- State when a movement may need professional modification.

### Food and allergy content

- Allergies are hard exclusions, not preferences.
- Do not suggest “small amounts” of an allergen.
- Distinguish allergy, intolerance, preference, and religious/cultural restriction.
- Warn that packaged-food labels and cross-contamination rules vary by country.

## 6. Evidence Rules

### Source hierarchy

1. Systematic reviews, meta-analyses, consensus statements, and major public-health guidance.
2. High-quality randomized or longitudinal human studies.
3. Well-designed observational studies.
4. Mechanistic, animal, or preliminary studies, clearly labeled.
5. Expert interpretation, only when its limits are explicit.

### Every evidence-based article must include

- Claim.
- Evidence strength: strong, moderate, limited, mixed, or unknown.
- Population and context.
- Practical implication.
- Limitation or uncertainty.
- Citation with authors, title, journal/organization, year, and stable URL/DOI when available.
- Review date.

### Citation prohibitions

- Never invent a study, statistic, DOI, author, journal, or URL.
- Never cite a commercial blog as primary evidence.
- Never use a citation to imply stronger evidence than it supports.
- If source verification is unavailable, write a non-specific educational explanation and mark it for review instead of fabricating a citation.

## 7. Content Scopes

### Articles and Learn

**Purpose:** Build understanding and trustworthy habits.

**Default structure:**

1. Useful title without clickbait.
2. One-sentence summary.
3. Why it matters.
4. Three to five short sections.
5. What the evidence says.
6. Practical options.
7. Limitations and safety note.
8. Three to five takeaways.
9. References.

**Target length:** 700–1,200 words for a full article; 250–500 words for a daily brief.

**Required metadata:** `id`, `title`, `excerpt`, `category`, `readTime`, `reviewedAt`, `evidenceLevel`, `sections`, `takeaways`, `references`, `safetyNote`.

**Categories:** nutrition, food skills, fasting, exercise, recovery, sleep, mindfulness, stress, behavior change, body literacy, privacy.

### Recipes and meal plans

**Purpose:** Make nutritious eating practical, flexible, affordable, and culturally broad.

Every recipe must include:

- Ingredients with quantities and units.
- Servings, preparation time, cooking time, and storage notes.
- Calories and macros as estimates, not laboratory truth.
- Dietary tags, explicit allergen tags, and substitution rules.
- Cuisine or cultural context without stereotyping.
- Instructions with food-safety basics.
- Cost tier and equipment needs.

Never invent nutrition precision for unknown brands. Use ranges or mark values as estimates.

### Workouts

**Purpose:** Help users train consistently and safely at their current level.

Every exercise or session must include:

- Goal, level, duration, equipment, and primary muscles.
- Setup and execution cues.
- Sets, reps or time, rest, and progression rule.
- Easier and harder alternatives.
- Stop/modify guidance.
- Media source and license/attribution metadata.

Programs must support 10, 20, 30, 45, and 60 minutes and home/gym/no-equipment variants.

### Mindful

**Purpose:** Offer grounded moments of attention, breathing, recovery, and self-compassion.

Use invitational language: “notice,” “try,” “allow,” “return.” Avoid spiritual certainty, mental-health diagnosis, trauma claims, or promises of instant calm.

Every script includes duration, intention, steps, closing prompt, and a note to stop if uncomfortable.

### Coaching and AI guidance

**Purpose:** Turn user-selected data into clear, optional next steps.

Output order:

1. What the user shared.
2. What can reasonably be inferred.
3. One or two options.
4. Tradeoffs.
5. A small next step.
6. Uncertainty/safety note.

AI must ask before changing logs, plans, targets, or reminders. AI must not diagnose, prescribe, or claim to know more than the user provided.

### Onboarding and UI copy

**Purpose:** Reduce cognitive load and collect only useful information.

- Explain why each field is requested.
- Show units beside every value.
- Use examples, not ambiguous placeholders.
- Mark optional fields clearly.
- Never make users disclose sensitive information to access local features.
- Use error copy that tells the user how to recover.

## 8. Model Output Contracts

### General content request

Return strict JSON when requested by the application. Do not include markdown fences.

```json
{
  "title": "",
  "summary": "",
  "audience": "beginner|intermediate|mixed",
  "category": "",
  "content": "",
  "takeaways": [],
  "safetyNote": "",
  "evidenceLevel": "strong|moderate|limited|mixed|unknown",
  "references": [],
  "reviewFlags": []
}
```

`reviewFlags` must identify missing source verification, medical sensitivity, unsupported numbers, allergen uncertainty, licensing uncertainty, or cultural assumptions.

### Prompt envelope for other models

```text
You are writing for Aurashape. Follow docs/CONTENT-VOICE-AND-SCOPE.md.
Audience: [audience]
Format: [article|daily brief|recipe|workout|mindful script|coach guidance|UI copy]
Goal: [user outcome]
Inputs: [only user-approved inputs]
Evidence requirement: [citations required|practical guidance only]
Safety context: [relevant exclusions or contraindications]
Length: [target]
Return: [JSON contract or prose structure]
Never invent citations, medical certainty, nutrition precision, or media licenses.
```

## 9. Review Gates

Every generated item passes these checks before publication:

1. Voice: calm, clear, warm, non-judgmental, practical.
2. Accuracy: claims match cited evidence and uncertainty is visible.
3. Safety: no diagnosis, prescription, dangerous restriction, shame, or unsupported promise.
4. Personalization: allergies, units, level, access, and user preferences are respected.
5. Accessibility: headings, short paragraphs, labels, readable contrast, and meaningful empty states.
6. Licensing: media and references have a source and allowed usage status.
7. Localization readiness: avoid idioms, unexplained units, and culture-specific assumptions.
8. Product behavior: content has stable IDs, version/review dates, and a safe fallback if remote data fails.

## 10. Content Roadmap

### First batch

- 50 Learn articles across the ten categories above.
- 120 recipes with explicit allergen metadata.
- 40 exercises with complete instructional metadata and lawful media sources, then expand the existing exercise catalog.
- 24 mindful scripts across six intentions and four durations.
- 30 onboarding/help messages for consent, units, fasting, goals, and projections.

### Quality bar

Content is not complete because a model generated it. It is complete when it is structured, cited where required, safety-reviewed, license-reviewed, testable, and usable without the generating model.
